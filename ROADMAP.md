# The Ever-Unfolding Saga — Roadmap

This is the authoritative overview of unfinished work, current priorities, and planned milestones. It should be possible to understand what remains without searching the source tree or relying on open GitHub issues.

Completed work in the current development line belongs in [CHANGELOG.md](CHANGELOG.md); earlier completed work belongs in [CHANGELOG-HISTORY.md](CHANGELOG-HISTORY.md). Detailed design decisions live in [docs/design/](docs/design/README.md), while [system documentation](docs/systems/) describes current implementation behavior and boundaries.

## Cross-cutting release operations

The CI/CD workflow supports artifact-only publication of an existing tag to GitHub Releases, itch.io, or both. It reuses the exact desktop and Android workflow artifacts produced by the tag-triggered build runs, verifies the complete eight-file set, and does not rebuild or re-sign. This manual promotion is available only while the original workflow artifacts remain within their seven-day retention period.

## Current development line — v0.53.x: world topology and spatial-world expansion

**Repository version at this roadmap revision: 0.53.30.**

**Goal:** extend the local spatial prototype into a persistent world whose connected locations and regions have coherent, deterministic foundations.

**Visual realization status:** new generic outdoor maps now project each location's persisted macro-geography footprint into local terrain. Ocean, inland lakes, and the new local river channel bands become impassable water; coast/desert samples become sand, and alpine/strong mountain-ridge samples become rocky rubble; other land remains open floor. River channels are continuous deterministic curves derived from shared watershed links and appear as provisional tinted water tiles. New route plans retain exact sub-sample river-bank intersections and now also retain stable lake identity and exact entry/exit shoreline points for land-to-land ferry crossings. Water and sand still reuse the floor texture with material tints. The local-map generator version advances from 9 to 11 across v0.53.26-v0.53.27; established maps remain unchanged. New generic outdoor maps now place walkable local fords and bridges across river components when both banks fit within the map; dedicated crossing artwork and route-specific local infrastructure placement remain unfinished. Lake crossings with verified shores expose a ferry scene; verified river crossings use route-specific ford/bridge scenes oriented from persisted bank pairs. Open-ocean and unresolved or incomplete water crossings are blocked for physical and known-route travel. Dedicated crossing artwork and infrastructure ownership/construction remain unfinished. Detailed shorelines/elevation, vegetation, mountain silhouettes, dedicated water/terrain assets, and underground geology also remain unfinished. Existing sky/time presentation, player and actor sprites, material palette, and provisional route/cave-connection tints remain. See [macro-geography](docs/systems/macro-geography.md) for the implemented-versus-rendered boundary and remaining visual work.

**Current local-map scale baseline:** newly generated outdoor maps now default to 64×48 tiles (up from 24×16), while compact interiors remain smaller where appropriate (the authored shrine chamber uses 32×24). World Lab offers 64×48, 80×56, and 96×64 presets. Existing saved maps are never resized. This is an initial increase, not the final scale target: outdoor areas should take several minutes to cross, locations may span multiple local areas, and regions must be much larger than one local map. See [world scale and travel](docs/design/world-scale-and-travel.md).

**Added in this iteration:** built on the merged river-channel work with walkable local ford/bridge spans, exact persisted Q16 bank intersections for river and inland-lake crossings, and a route-aware ferry landing/cutscene sequence for lake crossings with verified shores. Existing river outlet identities, lake IDs, width classes, flow directions, and old route records remain authoritative; no established route or local map is silently rewritten.

**Delivered in v0.53.30:** route-specific river ford/bridge scenes use persisted Q16 bank intersections and infrastructure classification, while resolved lakes retain their ferry scenes. Mixed routes sequence verified crossings in travel order. Open ocean, unresolved/survey-required water, lakes without verified shores, and new rivers without verified banks block physical and known-route fast travel. Fast travel can choose an alternate explored route; legacy FordOrBridge records remain traversable. Visuals remain transient colored placeholders, with no save-format or local-map generator bump.

**Delivered in v0.53.29:** a physical route passage backed by a lake crossing with two verified shores now acts as a ferry landing. Using it plays a short boarding, crossing, and landing sequence for each resolved lake on the route, in the direction travelled, before applying the existing world-route travel. The placeholder scene uses colored shoreline, water, and boat shapes; it derives horizontal/vertical crossing orientation from the persisted Q16 shore pair. Player movement and other spatial input are suspended during the sequence. This is transient presentation state: no save-format, geography-ruleset, or local-map generator bump is required. Dedicated ferry art, richer boat movement, and broader infrastructure/travel enforcement remain future work.

**Delivered in v0.53.28:** planned lake crossings now retain a stable generated lake ID plus exact Q16 entry and exit shores when a bounded route path enters and leaves the same inland lake over dry land. The crossing coordinate is the rounded midpoint of the two shores. Lake shore sampling follows every node of the bounded route path; it does not add whole-world searches or lake simulation. Legacy route records default the new optional metadata to empty, with no save-format or geography-ruleset bump. At the v0.53.28 stage, shore pairs were route metadata only; v0.53.29 now uses resolved pairs for the initial ferry landing and crossing scene. Dedicated ferry artwork and broader route travel enforcement remain future work.

**Delivered in v0.53.27:** new geography-backed outdoor maps identify connected local river-channel components and place at most one deterministic walkable crossing per component when both banks are present. Narrow/medium rivers use ford tiles and wide/very-wide rivers use bridge tiles, with provisional stone/wood material tints. Crossing tiles participate in ordinary movement, pathfinding, interaction line of sight, and reachable placement. Incomplete channels and lakes/ocean remain impassable. The local-map generator advances from 10 to 11; established saves and authored layouts are not regenerated. Exact association between these local crossings and individual routes' persisted macro bank coordinates, ferry presentation, and dedicated crossing assets remain future work.

**Delivered in v0.53.26:** derived deterministic curved channel strips from adjacent watershed-cell drainage links, with shared endpoints, relative width bands, and bounded local tile sampling. Newly generated generic outdoor maps now render river channels as impassable tinted water without turning whole watershed cells into rivers. New route plans record fixed-point entry/exit positions at the exact generated banks when a path crosses both sides. Older route records load without bank data, and existing saved maps and established route paths are not silently rewritten. The local-map generator version advances from 9 to 10; established maps are not regenerated. Physical ford/bridge/ferry structures and dedicated water artwork remain future work.

**Delivered in v0.53.25:** added deterministic, bounded river-course tracing through the existing coarse watershed network. River traces expose ordered macro-coordinate centre-points, downhill flow direction, outlet-based stable identities shared by tributaries reaching the same outlet, and relative widths derived from contributing area. New route-crossing candidates store optional river identity, width, and flow direction, distinguishing natural ford requirements for narrower rivers from bridge requirements for wider rivers; lakes require ferry transport, open ocean is impassable to overland routes, and unresolved water remains a survey requirement. Existing route-crossing records remain readable, and established route paths are not silently replanned.

**Delivered in v0.53.24:** added a renderer-neutral, bounded projection from location macro footprints into local-map terrain. New generic outdoor maps now show open ground, impassable ocean/lake water, sandy coast/desert areas, and rocky alpine/mountain areas. The initial entry and discovery remain within one reachable land component. Existing saved maps and authored layouts are not regenerated; detailed river, vegetation, mountain, shoreline, water, and dedicated terrain art remain future work.

**Delivered in v0.53.23:** added optional authored local-map exit-side preferences keyed by stable destination content IDs. Authored compass-side preferences override macro-route headings for newly placed passages; exact positions remain generated, safe, reachable, and deterministic.

**Delivered in v0.53.22:** added data-driven `settlement`, `wilderness`, and `landmark` location placement archetypes. Settlement candidates favor flatter land and nearby classified freshwater; wilderness retains broad environmental flexibility; landmarks favor elevated, geologically distinctive terrain. Existing anchors and explicit authored coordinates remain unchanged, while legacy worlds without anchors adopt the new policy when embedded.

**Delivered in v0.53.21:** introduced a restrained material-led provisional palette for the existing local-map floor, ash, rubble, wall, pillar, and table sprites. Discovery shading remains distinct, and existing route/actor semantic tints are retained. This is a renderer-only interim treatment; biome-specific vegetation, water, and regional environmental colours await corresponding local-map terrain/art coverage.

**Delivered in v0.53.0:** introduced authoritative persistent route records with stable route identities and explicit directionality; migrated legacy location adjacency during save loading without consuming entity IDs; linked local-map passages to route IDs; and added non-destructive passage reconciliation for established maps.

**Delivered in v0.53.1:** established signed global macro-sample and region-chunk coordinates, fixed the region resolution at 256 × 256 samples, added deterministic coordinate-derived sample seeds and geography-ruleset metadata, and assigned stable coordinates to newly generated regions. Legacy worlds remain unembedded for the separately planned compatibility migration.

**Delivered in v0.53.2:** added a deterministic fixed-point surface elevation field with continent-scale, regional, and detail noise, producing connected landmasses and smaller coastal/island variation on the shared global sample lattice.

**Delivered in v0.53.3:** added deterministic regional temperature and moisture fields, elevation cooling, ocean-influenced moisture, precipitation potential, and renderer-independent surface biome classification. The initial catalogue covers ocean, coast, desert, grassland, temperate forest, taiga, tundra, and alpine environments.

**Delivered in v0.53.4:** added explicit, direction-aware shoreline samples for both land and water, derived from neighboring global elevation samples and shared by climate biome classification.

**Delivered in v0.53.5:** added a hierarchical watershed foundation with coarse downstream links, local within-cell D8 drainage, and bounded tracing to ocean or closed inland outlets.

**Delivered in v0.53.6:** added deterministic, bounded upstream contributing-area estimates and river-channel classification on the coarse watershed network. Queries inspect at most 128 contributing cells and explicitly report truncated estimates.

**Delivered in v0.53.7:** added bounded depression analysis for closed inland watershed outlets. A deterministic minimax search resolves the lowest local spill elevation and connected below-spill footprint within a fixed 17 × 17 coarse-cell window; it describes terrain basin potential without claiming persistent lake placement or simulated water volume.

**Delivered in v0.53.8:** extended bounded reverse-watershed accumulation to sum each contributing coarse cell's deterministic precipitation potential alongside contributing area. The exposed Q16 × cell value is a relative index, not physical discharge; existing channel classification remains area-based, and truncated sums are explicitly lower bounds.

**Delivered in v0.53.9:** added deterministic inland-lake coverage from connected closed-basin footprints, precipitation-scaled fill levels, and stable basin-derived IDs. Added optional world-persisted generic modifications keyed by those IDs, with backward-compatible defaults for existing saves. River inflow is exposed as an initial outlet-channel indicator; physical discharge and connected water simulation remain future work.

**Delivered in v0.53.10:** added a renderer-independent surface-water sampler that composes ocean, inland-lake, and river-channel classification. River samples expose their immediate downstream cell and classify the destination as ocean, lake, river, lower-threshold land, an inconclusive truncated estimate, or a closed basin, reusing the existing downhill watershed graph rather than introducing a second routing simulation.

**Delivered in v0.53.11:** added deterministic, warped ridged mountain belts whose bounded uplift contributes to the shared surface elevation field. Climate and watershed systems therefore sample the same mountain-shaped terrain. Geography ruleset 2 enables this field while ruleset 1 preserves the original elevation contract.

**Delivered in v0.53.12:** exposed the existing mountain-ridge strength and barrier classification through the shared surface-terrain sample, allowing future route and location-placement systems to evaluate mountain crossings without duplicating sampling. Ridge strength is a soft crossing-resistance signal; weaker corridors can guide candidate mountain-pass placement. Water crossing constraints remain distinct through the existing hydrology sampler. No geography ruleset or save-format change was required.

**Delivered in v0.53.13:** added deterministic underground cave-network sampling on negative geography strata. Sparse chamber nodes expose stable coordinate identities and reciprocal cardinal passage links; samples distinguish solid rock, chambers, and passages. Queries are local and bounded, use the shared signed global lattice, and remain continuous across region boundaries. Generated cave geometry is derived and requires no save migration.

**Delivered in v0.53.14:** integrated macro-geography with persistent location anchors and footprints, deterministic legacy-world embedding, and bounded physical corridor assessment. Existing region coordinates remain unchanged; legacy regions without coordinates are embedded using saved route-graph structure and stable IDs. Legacy geography seeds derive from immutable saved identities when no generation seed exists. New location anchors are deterministic within their owning region and authored content may specify exact anchors/footprint radii. Candidate route corridors expose distinct water and mountain-resistance signals without changing graph connectivity or authorizing crossings.

**Delivered in v0.53.15:** added bounded deterministic A* route planning over the macro-geography lattice. Persisted route records now retain canonical paths, broad profiles, planning status, cost, and explicit river/lake/unresolved-water/mountain crossing requirements. New local-map passages inherit route profiles and prefer the macro path's compass heading; established passage identities and positions remain unchanged.

**Delivered in v0.53.16:** added on-demand cardinal frontier passages whose stable identities derive from the source location's persisted geography key and adjacent region coordinates. A frontier creates no destination while merely being listed or displayed. When the player uses one, the system selects deterministic land anchors near the shared region edge, requires a bounded planned land route, then creates or reuses exactly the adjacent region and one destination location. The canonical route and geographic path are persisted, and the exact pending local-map passage is converted in place without changing its passage ID or position. Repeated materialization reuses the same region, location, and route; it does not generate neighboring regions or reroll established world/map state.

**Delivered in v0.53.17:** added bounded, deterministic vertical-connection sampling between adjacent strata. Surface entrances require exposed land directly above open cave space; underground shafts require both adjacent strata to contain open cave space at the same global horizontal coordinate. Each cave network cell exposes at most one stable connection candidate per layer pair, and nearby queries are bounded to at most 25 cells.

**Delivered in v0.53.18:** introduced persistent, individually addressable cave-chamber locations. Cave locations use stable geography-derived keys and anchors; only immediate reciprocal horizontal passages and valid adjacent-stratum shafts are exposed as connections. Materializing a connection creates or reuses only its destination chamber and owning region, then persists one bidirectional authoritative route. Reverse surface-shaft traversal reuses the original surface endpoint. Generated cave geometry remains derived, and materialization is idempotent.

**Delivered in v0.53.19:** integrated cave topology into local spatial maps. Surface entrances, chamber-to-chamber passages, and vertical shafts are placed as reachable interior transitions rather than artificial map-edge exits. Pending transitions materialize their authoritative cave location and route only when used, then upgrade the same physical entrance in place. Existing saved maps are migrated on entry by adding deterministic cave transitions while retaining established terrain, discoveries, props, encounters, and route passages.

## Following milestone — v0.54.x: world content, simulation, character lives, and action combat

**Goal:** make the expanded world more coherent and reactive, and let individual character lives create lasting, comprehensible consequences.

- Build deterministic, context-aware procedural content generation for regional and location identity, landmarks, resources, inhabitants, factions, discoveries, quests, and opportunities. Authored content and mods retain precedence; locations need identity, not mandatory loot or encounters.
- Extend mod support with schema-validated, mod-authored procedural templates and archetypes, stable-ID-based additions/overrides, and clear precedence with authored campaign content. Defer arbitrary scripted hooks until a concrete design justifies their complexity.
- Strengthen the authoritative world-time and simulation contract. Support detailed, event-driven, aggregate, and deferred updates according to gameplay relevance, with explicit state ownership, stable ordering, persistence, and duplicate prevention.
- Add lightweight, time-driven regional climate evolution: seasonal wet/dry patterns, rainfall variation, drought, and structured consequences for settlement water availability, crops/vegetation, and travel. Let rivers and lakes respond to broad wet/dry periods without implementing physical discharge calibration or continuous water simulation.
- Extend character progression into meaningful choices, optional backgrounds and ambitions, permanent life records, retirement into persistent NPCs, and continuity rules separating world state, recorded knowledge, and individual knowledge.
- Design and introduce real-time spatial action combat as a deliberate replacement path for the current turn-based encounter model. Establish attack phases, Strong/Fast/Group styles, target handling, assisted positioning, combos, block/parry, injury consequences, structured combat events, and desktop/Android controls. Develop bespoke combat HUD elements and combat-specific art alongside this replacement system rather than polishing the transitional encounter UI in advance.
- Profile representative desktop and Android workloads before deciding simulation limits or presentation budgets. Do not impose arbitrary ceilings on future simulation depth.

Design references: [procedural content generation](docs/design/procedural-content-generation.md), [world simulation](docs/design/world-simulation.md), [character lives and inheritance](docs/design/character-lives-and-inheritance.md), and [real-time spatial combat](docs/design/real-time-spatial-combat.md).

## Later milestone — v0.55.x: social power, livelihoods, and command

**Goal:** turn relationships, resources, and preparation into practical ways to act on and influence the persistent world.

- Establish target-specific loyalty and trust as independent relationship axes; distinguish voluntary commitment, contractual cooperation, fear-induced compliance, coercion, and force.
- Add persistent agreements, recruitment, assignments, group membership, leadership, shared resources, and organizations that can outlive a character.
- Build a small connected livelihood loop using shared resources and inventory across gathering, productive work, crafting, alchemy, and trade.
- Make preparation meaningful through equipment, consumables, information, allies, and supplies without imposing a mandatory continuous-needs-management loop.
- Extend spatial combat toward small-group tactical command, then larger group/organization behavior only when the shared systems support it.

Design references: [social influence and organizations](docs/design/social-influence-and-organizations.md) and [livelihoods, preparation, and command](docs/design/livelihoods-preparation-and-command.md).

## Dependencies and sequencing

- World topology/geography is the foundation for frontier expansion and location-aware procedural content.
- Procedural content builds on stable region/location identities and should materialize content idempotently rather than rebuilding the world.
- World simulation provides the time/update/persistence contract used by character lives, social systems, livelihoods, and world evolution.
- Character lives and inheritance depend on correct world-vs-character ownership and structured persistent consequences.
- Social systems and livelihood/command systems reuse the simulation and character-continuity foundations rather than creating separate management games.
- Real-time combat builds on spatial movement, actor, animation, and input foundations; audio consumes structured gameplay events without owning combat rules.

These version groupings express intended dependency order, not a promise that every bullet ships together. Split them into bounded implementation milestones as dependencies become ready. Update this file whenever a goal is completed, deferred, re-scoped, or superseded.
