# The Ever-Unfolding Saga

## Roadmap

The roadmap tracks current and upcoming development. Detailed completed milestone history is kept in [`docs/roadmap-history.md`](docs/roadmap-history.md). The intended product direction is recorded in [`docs/design-principles.md`](docs/design-principles.md); this roadmap distinguishes those long-term goals from what is currently implemented and validated.

## Current state

The v0.52.0–v0.52.6 storage, mod-management, and character-focused interface work is complete and recorded in the roadmap history. Spatial exploration and interaction are now the active development line. PR #404 implements persistent spatial actors, contextual action/target resolution, and the modular portrait catalogue. Its implementation commit passed automated checks; desktop and Android landscape playtesting is deferred until the spatial-first migration is complete. The player-anchored radial interaction wheel adapts to landscape viewport dimensions, adds a second ring for crowded targets, and animates selected-entry feedback. Shared scene transitions and the dedicated close-up dialogue presentation from #405 have been manually play-tested by the project owner. The player now configures a persistent portrait during new-character creation, while NPC portraits retain deterministic weighted generation; the spatial interaction control identifies its nearby target before opening that target's action list. The spatial world update uses mutually exclusive player, floor-tile, and wall-side sprite queries, preventing Bevy ECS access conflicts during scene startup.

## Spatial-first gameplay direction

The former Gameplay dashboard was a transitional hub and has now been retired. Spatial exploration is the sole in-session gameplay context. Its useful functions have been migrated into spatial HUD controls, contextual interactions, overlays, and focused record screens; the surviving navigation adapter owns only the pause menu and interim World Map. There is no dashboard screen or Leave transition. The spatial scene should remain the primary context when opening and closing ordinary overlays where appropriate; safe-location activities must remain convenient rather than requiring unnecessary movement. Location names are now announced briefly on entry or travel rather than kept in the persistent spatial header; the focused World Map is now accessible from the spatial menu and shows the current region/location context alongside known locations and graph-backed routes; its interim layout is topological only, with continuous geography-backed mapping still future work under #365. The graph is now hosted in a clipped two-dimensional viewport with current-location-centered opening, horizontal/vertical drag panning, Android pinch zoom, desktop cursor-centered wheel zoom, Fit, and Reset controls; destination activation is deferred until a completed tap and cancelled for drag/pinch gestures, while viewport state remains presentation-only. The dashboard/pause function audit and proposed spatial placements are recorded in [the spatial-first function map](docs/systems/spatial-first-migration.md). The unified pause menu is accessible over spatial exploration through a corner menu control, desktop Escape, and Android Back; dismissing it restores the same spatial session. The corner control replaces the former Leave action: tapping opens the pause menu, while a 650 ms touch hold without dragging opens the developer console; the desktop slash shortcut also works in spatial exploration. The spatial scene reserves a compact 72-logical-pixel opaque header at the top of the full window. Its upper row contains world time, character identity, health, conditions, and the pause/menu control; a short lower strip carries contextual status. A dedicated full-window UI camera keeps the header and overlays anchored to the screen while the spatial world camera renders only below the header and outer spacing. Eligible Interact/Investigate controls retain their lower-right map-footer placement, and the Android virtual joystick remains a movement control over the map. The former Gameplay dashboard, its renderer, and its navigation destination have been removed. Dedicated screens return to spatial exploration. The World Map now handles fast travel to previously visited locations through explored reciprocal routes; each hop uses the normal travel action. Persistent local maps remain the primary visit record, with a bounded-history fallback for older saves that predate local-map persistence. Visited locations without an available known route are visually distinct from unexplored frontier locations. Fast-travel destinations remain direct children of the graph canvas rather than being reparented into the shared vertical menu-action group; their warm-accent button state remains visible and interactive. Ordinary travel remains available through physical passages in spatial gameplay. Meditate appears in the same menu only when safe, opening the existing meditation flow and returning to the paused spatial scene. New character creation, inheritance, and successful save loading now hand off directly to spatial exploration.

The first foundation refinement makes movement collision checks resilient to brief frame drops and makes camera smoothing frame-rate independent while enforcing map bounds after smoothing. The spatial sprite-animation layer now consumes a mod-aware JSON manifest defining the 32×48 frame grid, four separate directional idle/walk pairs, per-clip frame ranges, durations, and looping behavior. Idle cycles advance while stationary; six-frame walking cycles remain movement-speed-scaled and frame-rate independent. The Bevy renderer derives atlas dimensions and animated sprite sizing from that configuration while retaining the existing 16×24 static fallback and unchanged collision footprint. The base sheet contract is a 192×384 PNG with four idle frames and six walk frames per direction. The bundled atlas now contains corrected north-facing artwork in rows 0–1 and south-facing artwork in rows 4–5, with the existing east- and west-facing rows retained. The magenta source background is transparent and the sheet was downsampled with nearest-neighbor sampling; in-game clip playback and alignment are awaiting manual validation tracked under #446. Dedicated screens retain their invoking screen as a return target, and returning to spatial exploration rebuilds its presentation from the same map and continuous position. This is an incremental foundation pass, not a claim that the spatial-first migration is complete.

The interim World Map uses a renderer-neutral view of stable location/region identities, exploration state, fast-travel eligibility, and authoritative route edges. It shows the current region and location, marks previously visited places, and exposes the immediate frontier around known locations. Selecting a visited place fast-travels over reciprocal connections between visited locations, invoking the normal travel action for each hop and stopping at consequential arrival events or danger. Unvisited frontier locations require ordinary spatial travel through physical passages. The graph layout is recalculated for presentation and is not stored in world data or saves; the separate destination-only Travel screen has been removed. See [World Map design and replacement boundaries](docs/systems/world-map.md); continuous, coherent macro-geography remains future work under #365.

## Developer tools

**v0.52.21 — Developer tools: World Lab authoring completion (#413)**

The start screen exposes a Developer Tools hub without requiring a campaign or save. Character Lab manually assembles loaded modular portrait parts, previews them through the same layered portrait compositor and mod-aware PNG loader used by dialogue, and supports per-slot selection, visibility toggles, deterministic randomization, reset, and diagnostics. Each portrait slot has adjacent previous/next controls aligned with its body region; these use the same viewer and input semantics as new-character creation. The detail pane is touch-scrollable so longer part lists and diagnostics remain reachable. This remains a portrait-focused tool, not a full-body character editor.

World Lab generates reproducible temporary local maps from a deterministic, disposable world graph and uses the same spatial generator and gameplay renderer as exploration. Its editor supports validated terrain changes; pillar/table placement, movement, and removal; temporary point-of-interest placement, movement, and removal; physical passage placement, movement, and removal; and actor placement, movement, and removal. Passage destinations are selected from real locations in the generated world graph, and edits update reciprocal graph links. Actor placements reference actual generated NPC/remains entity identities and never invent entity IDs. Placement validation protects entries, walkable paths, discoveries, passages, actors, and prop footprints from conflicting edits. Maps can be play-tested through the production spatial renderer, movement/collision, depth ordering, camera follow, and Android virtual joystick; edits survive returning to the editor and remain isolated from campaign state and saves. Versioned JSON fixtures preserve complete LocalMap state, can be imported/exported through desktop and Android document pickers, and are validated before loading. Fixture restoration reconstructs the deterministic world graph and actor catalogue from the saved seed, retaining only passage and actor references that still resolve. Fixtures use a dedicated developer-fixtures directory and import inbox, never campaign save slots; invalid files remain visible with diagnostics.

Character Lab manually assembles currently available modular portrait parts using the same gameplay compositor and mod-aware asset loader as dialogue. Its per-slot arrows align with each body region in the shared character appearance viewer, while its details pane scrolls on touch and pointer input. Headgear and accessory can be explicitly set to none, and the deterministic randomizer can omit either optional layer. Shared presentation state now restores scroll positions when a screen rebuilds its scrollable panels. Character Lab also supports visibility toggles, deterministic randomization, reset, and diagnostics. This completes the initial developer-tools scope; full-body character authoring is not a prerequisite because the required full-body visual assets and renderer do not yet exist. Future Character Lab expansion can be tracked separately when those systems arrive. The Developer Tools view now remains mounted until its own screen or presentation state changes; unrelated spatial-state change detection no longer repeatedly destroys and recreates its interactive controls.

## Active milestone

**v0.52.35 — Directional player idle/walk animation and asset contract** (prototype #353; actor and interaction foundation #404; presentation follow-up #405; world-topology design #365)

The renderer-neutral local-map model, deterministic generation, Bevy exploration view, continuous movement, blocked-terrain collision, camera tracking, depth ordering, and world-persistent map state are implemented. The reusable player animation layer now reads its clip layout and timing from a mod-aware JSON manifest, supports four-frame directional idle cycles and six-frame directional walk cycles, and keeps the character grounded while using 32×48 frames. The matching 192×384 PNG is now bundled with corrected north-facing rows 0–1 and south-facing rows 4–5. Its magenta source background is transparent and the full atlas was downsampled with nearest-neighbor sampling. The static fallback remains available if the optional animation assets fail validation; in-game directional playback and alignment are awaiting manual validation under #446. The spatial header reserves its own opaque region above the world camera viewport and groups world time, character identity, health, conditions, and contextual notices without obscuring the map. Its corner menu control replaces the former Leave action; tapping opens the unified pause menu, while a 650 ms touch hold without dragging opens the developer console. The desktop slash shortcut is also available in spatial exploration. The normal spatial header has no redundant heading; its compact time indicator stands on its own, while location names appear only in short arrival notices. The normal spatial flow no longer offers a transition to the compatibility Gameplay dashboard. The focused World Map is available from the spatial menu; its interim graph shows the current location, previously visited places, and visible frontier exits. Selecting a visited place fast-travels along known reciprocal routes, applying the normal travel action at each hop and pausing for danger or arrival events. Ordinary travel remains spatial, through physical passages. Its graph positions and viewport pan/zoom state are presentation-only. The clipped viewport supports free panning, touch pinch zoom, desktop drag/wheel navigation, Fit, and Reset while keeping the existing graph and fast-travel rules authoritative. Canvas-positioned fast-travel buttons are excluded from shared vertical action grouping, with regression coverage for entity parenting and preservation of absolute coordinates. Map status labels distinguish visited locations from unexplored frontier nodes. Runtime UI labels and controls use ASCII separators and symbols to avoid unsupported-font glyphs. Manual Android and desktop interaction validation remains pending. The continuous geography-backed landscape remains future work under #365. The prototype includes desktop keyboard movement, an Android virtual joystick, first-pass pixel-art terrain and character assets, basic prop collision and occlusion, and the authored Old Shrine chamber layout (#381). The exact active exploration position and session remain character-specific, while established local maps and their lasting changes persist with the world.

PR #404 extends Explore to safe and dangerous locations and removes detached Talk and Search Remains dashboard actions. NPCs and searchable remains receive deterministic placements on reachable map cells and persist with their local maps; older saves receive safe defaults until a map is first entered. Nearby NPCs, remains, points of interest, and passages resolve into stable action/target pairs through renderer-neutral discovery in `interaction_actions.rs`; the Bevy adapter consumes those results rather than owning proximity, line-of-sight, actor-state, and inventory eligibility rules. The player-anchored radial action wheel presents every eligible pair, adapts to available landscape space, uses a second ring when nearby targets crowd the first, and gives selection changes a brief eased pulse. Directional keyboard selection and Android touch activation use the visible entry positions, and selected identities are revalidated before dispatching the existing Talk, Search Remains, Examine, or Enter behavior. New local maps can also receive multiple deterministic, campaign-authored points of interest; they remain hidden until their map tiles are explored, then appear as world markers and eligible Examine actions. Their reveal and examination state persists with the local map, and mods can extend or override definitions by stable ID. NPC dialogue now transitions into a dedicated close-up scene showing the player and NPC together. The player's portrait is composed from stable, player-selected body, face, hair, headgear, and accessory part IDs configured during new-character creation; the Appearance tab's four controls dispatch their exact part/slot selection directly, preserving its nonzero selection range; NPC portraits continue to use deterministic weighted selection from the same mod-extensible catalogue. The shared transition primitive animates scene entry and exit while the spatial session remains mounted, and the mod-aware loader supplies the selected portrait PNGs with a text fallback when portrait assets are unavailable.

End-to-end desktop and Android landscape validation of the migrated pause menu, World Map, meditation, investigation, character records, lifecycle, and return-navigation flows remains part of #421. Manual visual validation of the spatial renderer, authored Old Shrine chamber, and directional player animation is tracked separately under #446. The #405 interaction presentation includes the animated radial wheel with responsive crowded-target layout and eased selection feedback, reusable scene-root transitions, and the close-up two-character dialogue scene with composited portrait layers. The project owner has manually play-tested this work and confirmed it behaves as intended; the player-portrait and target-first prompt refinements complete the remaining #405 feedback. The first authored spatial-discovery slice now covers Old Shrine with the Black Altar and Fresh Soot points of interest. Physical passages now invoke the existing graph-travel rules and transition directly into connected destination maps without leaving spatial mode. The current map and character session are persisted before travel; destination maps are restored from world state or generated lazily on first arrival. The spatial HUD now places Investigate beside Interact only while an active threat belongs to the current location, then opens the existing Combat screen through `bevy_combat::begin`; the spatial frontend does not create a separate threat or combat ruleset. Spatial time presentation now reuses the authoritative 12-part world clock and existing mod-aware sun/moon artwork in a compact horizon indicator with a 24-hour readout. Dawn and dusk add restrained warm colour washes, while night adds a restrained cool/dim wash beneath the spatial HUD; the same day/time state drives both the cue and the environment. New character creation, inheritance, and successful save loading now enter spatial exploration directly. Character record regression coverage verifies that all five tabs and nested detail/journal Back paths retain the spatial invoking context. Frontier-region creation, known-route fast travel, expanded room topology, additional authored layouts, and room-aware camera transitions remain future work under #365. Richer multi-step clue chains, evidence collection, investigation outcomes, and dynamic encounter integration also remain future work.

Keep the existing world model, quests, time, narrative, and location-level actions authoritative. Spatial exploration is the primary gameplay shell and must continue to integrate those systems rather than replacing them. Real-time spatial combat and audio remain outside this milestone.

## Longer-term direction

Continue modularizing the codebase by responsibility rather than by file size alone. Keep runtime state, content loading, gameplay actions, presentation, persistence, and event processing independently understandable and testable.

The product direction is a character-focused, graphics-first spatial RPG backed by a persistent simulation. Spatial exploration is being delivered incrementally; real-time spatial action combat and audio integration remain future work, not current milestone claims. Keep world systems character-centred rather than turning them into remote grand-strategy management. Desktop and mobile support, hybrid input, scalable visual/simulation quality, offline single-player, player-controlled updates, and robust save/mod compatibility remain guiding constraints. See the [design principles](docs/design-principles.md).

Major gameplay work should remain data-driven where practical, preserve backward compatibility, and include focused tests for behavior affected by the change.

## Release distribution

GitHub Release publishing can optionally mirror all five desktop builds and three Android APKs to itch.io through Butler. The Android release workflow waits for the complete set of release assets before uploading them to their corresponding platform/architecture channels. Enable the mirror by configuring the repository Actions variable `ITCHIO_TARGET` and secret `BUTLER_API_KEY`; without both values, GitHub Release publishing continues without the itch.io upload.
