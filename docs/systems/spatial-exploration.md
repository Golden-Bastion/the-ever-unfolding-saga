# Spatial Exploration

## Responsibility boundaries

`spatial.rs` owns renderer-neutral local-map data, terrain walkability and prop footprints, explicit room-cell membership, grid-edge interior doors with open/closed/locked state, stable point-of-interest identity and evidence prerequisite metadata, deterministic generation, content-selected built-in room layouts, map exits, physical discovery state, persistent `SpatialEntityPlacement` records for world-owned NPCs/corpses, and the character's logical exploration session. `model.rs` owns each character's learned evidence IDs, while quest objectives reference those stable IDs. It also places campaign-authored discoveries deterministically when a new local map is generated. It must not depend on Bevy rendering or UI types. Room and door fields default to empty collections when absent, so saves created before this topology was introduced remain loadable. Existing maps keep their saved terrain and layout; topology is not inferred or regenerated during loading.

`interaction_actions.rs` owns renderer-neutral action/target identities and discovers all currently eligible contextual interactions from the local map and authoritative game state. It applies the established projected interaction radius, prop-aware line-of-sight checks, actor/inventory validation, deterministic candidate ordering, and stable action/target identity without depending on Bevy types. `bevy_spatial.rs` adapts that model to the Bevy frontend. It owns angled projection, terrain, prop and actor-marker rendering, depth ordering, movement input from keyboard and touch controls, collision checks, camera follow, wheel presentation, and synchronization with the active game state. Actor placements are added deterministically on map entry and only use reachable walkable cells. The saved world remains authoritative for persistent maps; the active character owns the exact continuous position. `bevy_interactions.rs` renders the corresponding dialogue and remains overlays without replacing the spatial scene. The spatial frontend also detects navigation re-entry after a dedicated screen has replaced its screen root and rebuilds the ECS presentation from the retained map and continuous player position; this does not regenerate the map or reset exploration progress.

## HUD and viewport

The spatial HUD uses an 88-logical-pixel opaque header anchored to the top of the full window. Its 64-pixel primary row holds the time-of-day indicator, the player-vitals panel, and the pause/menu control. The vitals panel stacks character identity and the condition summary above a full-width 20-pixel health gauge with numeric current/maximum values. The gauge width follows the available panel width rather than a fixed 100-pixel slot. A 20-pixel status strip below the primary row carries contextual feedback. The spatial world camera renders only inside a viewport beginning below the expanded header and outer spacing; a separate full-window UI camera keeps the header and overlays anchored to the actual screen rather than to the world viewport. Eligible Interact/Investigate controls retain their lower-right map-footer placement, and the Android virtual joystick remains anchored over the map as a movement control.

## Coordinate conventions

- Grid coordinates are continuous during movement. A tile's center is represented by `(x + 0.5, y + 0.5)`.
- Grid/world coordinates are authoritative for movement, collision, discovery, and persistence. Projected screen-space coordinates are presentation-only.
- The fixed three-quarter projection maps the two grid axes onto opposing diagonal screen directions. Input directions are converted through the inverse projection so keyboard and touch share the same movement rules.
- Sprite transforms are positioned from the ground-contact point plus a visual vertical offset. Actor depth follows the continuous ground position instead of snapping to a whole tile.
- Floor, ash, and rubble diamonds are placed on a dedicated background depth layer so ground surfaces cannot draw over actors. Wall tops are sorted from their ground-contact line; wall faces are sorted from their front/base edge. This lets the wall occlude a character behind it while a character moving beyond the wall's base renders in front. When an actor and wall top share the same ground depth, the actor wins the tie so a wall's entire top diamond does not hide a character in an adjacent lateral position.
- Wall tops extend upward beyond the floor diamond. Camera bounds account for that asymmetric vertical extent rather than expanding both vertical edges equally.

## Movement and interactions

Movement uses a small ground-space collision footprint and resolves the two grid axes separately, allowing movement to slide along a blocked edge. Frame movement is subdivided into short collision-checked steps (with a bounded frame-time contribution) so a brief low-frame-rate interval cannot skip across a wall or prop footprint. Walls and static prop footprints block movement; floor, ash, and rubble remain walkable. Pillars occupy one cell and tables occupy two adjacent cells in one grid row. Prop footprints are stored with the persistent local map, and the same walkability query is used for interaction line-of-sight checks.

Local spatial exploration is entered through Explore at both safe and dangerous locations. Location content may provide a `spatial_discoveries` list, each with a stable ID, title, and description. These definitions are used only when a local map is first generated: the prototype discovery is replaced by deterministically placed points on reachable walkable cells, spaced apart where the map permits and kept clear of exits and actors. A location with no authored list retains the legacy prototype fallback, which is removed at safe locations so it is not falsely presented as a real corpse. Existing saved maps remain authoritative and are never silently regenerated or repopulated when campaign definitions change. NPCs at the location and corpses with searchable inventory receive stable positions from reachable walkable cells. Existing positions are never rerolled on re-entry, and stale placements are harmless because rendering and targeting verify the actor's current location/state. Newly assigned positions avoid the entry, POIs, exits, props, walls, and other actor placements.

Each point of interest begins hidden. As the player explores, the existing revealed-tile area is checked against its position; entering that area reveals the point and displays its world marker. The local map persists the physical `discovered` and `interacted` flags. The current character separately persists the evidence IDs they have understood in `known_evidence_ids`; the map's interaction flag never grants knowledge by itself.

A discovered point offers an Examine action only when every authored prerequisite ID is already known by the current character and that character has not learned the point's own ID. Examining it records the stable ID on the character, writes the examination into world history, and synchronizes any matching quest objective. If a previous life examined the same physical site, a successor can still examine it and form their own understanding. A successor does not receive the predecessor's evidence list. This keeps lasting physical changes shared at world level while keeping deductions character-specific. Older maps default missing prerequisite lists to empty, and older characters default missing evidence lists to empty.

Hidden points do not appear in the action wheel and cannot be examined from a guessed position. The map's local geometry and the character's continuous position retain their existing persistence responsibilities.

The shipped opening route uses this rule across locations rather than as isolated local puzzles: the Ashen Gate evidence unlocks the next road clue, the road clue unlocks the disturbed stall and missing lantern wick at Hollow Market, and that finding unlocks the soot and altar evidence at Old Shrine. The `Quiet the Old Shrine` quest includes the final altar finding as an evidence objective. Since prerequisites use character-owned knowledge, a successor must retrace the evidence chain even when the physical sites were examined in an earlier life.

Contextual interaction discovery returns every eligible nearby NPC, corpse, POI, and edge passage as a renderer-neutral action/target pair. Each pair retains stable target identity, including individual POI and passage IDs; candidates are ordered by projected distance with deterministic tie-breaking. The Interact button and E/Enter input open a radial wheel anchored to the player's projected screen position. Every eligible pair appears as its own action entry, ordered by nearest projected distance; labels include the target where needed. Arrow keys or WASD move the selection toward the closest radial entry, Enter/E confirms, and Escape/Backspace closes. Android uses the same touchable entries and a central close control, with the movement joystick suspended while the wheel is open. The wheel adapts its diameter to the available landscape viewport, keeps all choices visible in a single ring or a compact second ring for crowded encounters, and anchors itself to the player's projected position. Opening and closing animate smoothly; changing the selected entry triggers a brief scale pulse in addition to its selected-state styling. The wheel never replaces the renderer-neutral action/target identity with a display index. The selected pair is re-resolved against current eligibility before execution, so range, actor existence, inventory, and clear-approach rules remain authoritative. Future combat must not treat this interaction range or line of sight as a substitute for attack collision or target eligibility.

Talking transitions from the spatial view into a dedicated close-up dialogue scene while preserving the active spatial session underneath. The scene places the player and NPC together, composes each portrait from the deterministic, renderer-neutral portrait layers, and presents memory, availability, responses, and choices in a dedicated dialogue area. Portrait PNGs are resolved through the same mod-aware asset loader as other game art; layers retain their declared draw order, missing parts are omitted, and the legacy NPC portrait text is shown only when no usable image layer can be loaded. A shared scene-root transition animates entry and exit. Movement remains suspended until the exit transition completes, then resumes at the exact previous exploration position. Searching a corpse retains its contextual inspection/recovery overlay and existing loot, hidden-discovery, and note presentation. Dialogue, time advancement, quest effects, and inventory mutation continue to use the authoritative game rules rather than renderer-owned logic.

## Persistent spatial encounters

`game/encounters.rs` groups campaign encounter profiles by their optional `spawn_group` when a local map is entered. Profiles within one group are weighted alternatives; each distinct group is evaluated independently and can materialize one record. Profiles without a group share the default group, preserving the original single-pool behavior. Eligible profiles are filtered by authored world conditions, their deterministic chance roll, and positive weight. The weighted selection uses a seed derived from the persistent map seed, location identity, world day, time, and (for non-default groups) the group identity. It does not consume the general gameplay RNG stream.

Each resulting `SpatialEncounter` record snapshots stable identity, linked quest ID, spawn group, position, enemy description, health, combat power, trophy, and lifecycle status into the local map. The local map remains authoritative after materialization: re-entry, save/load, or a later campaign/mod update does not reroll the selected enemy, reset its health, change its group, move it, or restore a defeated record. Existing records reserve their own groups, including after defeat. Groups that currently have no eligible profile remain unresolved and can materialize on a later map entry when their conditions become true. A map can therefore hold multiple independent encounter records, with one record per group and a distinct valid placement for each.

Nearby active records at the current location each supply their own renderer-neutral `EngageEncounter` action/target identity. The interaction wheel can present multiple nearby encounters as distinct targets, and combat startup resolves the selected stable record ID rather than assuming a single enemy per map. Range and line-of-sight validation remain authoritative, but confrontation no longer depends on the legacy global threat flag. The existing turn-based combat system still resolves each encounter and writes damage/defeat back to its persistent map record. The legacy global threat is retained for older location-name-only encounter definitions and unrelated world systems; victory clears it only when it belongs to the defeated encounter's location.

Older local maps that omit the optional `encounters` field deserialize with an empty collection. Legacy encounter records without group metadata belong to the default group, so they prevent a new default-group profile from replacing them while still allowing named groups to materialize. Older location-name-only campaign/mod profiles without the new spawn metadata also remain readable and keep their prior location-scoped threat requirement. Encounters remain stationary and do not respawn; roaming, awareness/pursuit, and off-screen AI simulation are future work. Real-time spatial action combat remains a separate future system governed by [the combat design](../design/real-time-spatial-combat.md).
## Authored room layouts

Campaign location content may specify the optional `spatial_layout` identifier. The built-in templates are:

- `shrine_chamber` (Old Shrine): a compact rectangular chamber with intersecting masonry walls, one open doorway, a pillar, and a table.
- `ashen_gate_causeway` (Ashen Gate): an ash-covered approach and dead road divided by the gate wall. The broken tower bases frame one open, closable threshold between the approach and the road beyond.
- `hollow_market_square` (Hollow Market): an ash-and-rubble market court with abandoned stall counters and unlit lantern posts. Existing table and pillar sprites are first-pass stand-ins until dedicated market props are authored.

Each layout is renderer-neutral. Terrain cells, prop footprints, room membership, and door edges feed the same movement, collision, pathfinding, and line-of-sight rules as procedural geometry. The Ashen Gate and Hollow Market layouts preserve their existing connected-location exits by routing generated edge passages through the authored threshold rather than cutting arbitrary openings through the room boundary. The gate and market templates require at least the standard 24×16 local-map dimensions; smaller maps safely use procedural generation instead. Hollow Market lists Old Shrine first and Ashen Gate second so the eastbound onward exit requires crossing the market threshold, while the return route remains on the entry side.

Layout selection affects newly generated maps only. Once saved, a local map's terrain, prop state, rooms, and doors remain authoritative; existing saved maps are not silently regenerated to apply a new layout. The local-map generator version advances when built-in generation changes, but loading an established map never reruns its layout. Older content without `spatial_layout` remains valid, and unrecognized layout identifiers fall back to the normal procedural map. Arbitrary mod-defined room geometry remains future work.

## Physical map exits

A local map's `exits` are derived from its world location's existing connected-location IDs. Each passage stores a stable target location ID rather than relying on a name or checking the destination's danger flag. Passage placement is deterministic, passages are positioned on edge tiles, and generated walkable corridors connect them to the map's entry area. Connection order in campaign content determines the stable compass-edge assignment (east, west, north, south, then repeated slots); authored location data can therefore put the primary onward route beyond a room threshold while keeping the return route near the entry.

A contextual passage action appears only when the character is within interaction range and has a clear approach. Pressing the contextual interaction key uses the nearest available passage, or examines the point of interest when no passage is in range. Directly activating a passage action applies the same range and approach validation.

Using a passage validates its stable destination ID against both ends of the active location's authoritative world-graph connection; the current route model requires reciprocal links and does not yet represent intentional one-way passages. It also checks the same range/clear-approach rules used by interaction discovery. A valid use goes through `game::actions::travel_to`, so the established route effects—world-time advancement, turn/history updates, quest synchronization, arrival events, and destination threat state—remain authoritative. The current local map and character's active session/position are persisted before travel. The destination map is then restored from persistent world state or generated lazily on first arrival, and the character is placed at that map's entry while remaining in spatial mode. The destination map's own graph-backed exits allow the player to continue between local areas. Spatial exploration remains the in-session gameplay context; dedicated screens return to it through the shared navigation state. This implements local graph-linked transitions; frontier-region creation remains future work, while known-route fast travel is available through the World Map.

Within the current map schema, `exits`, `props`, and `interactables` may be absent and default to empty collections; missing actor placements are added deterministically on entry. Pre-topology saves without `rooms` and `doors` remain compatible and load with empty topology. This preserves their established map geometry without inventing room boundaries or doors.

## Visual assets

The first spatial pixel-art set is packaged as protected base-game assets and includes wood-floor, ash, rubble, and wall-top tiles, separate left/right wall-face sprites, a character sprite, a passage marker, and dedicated pillar/table props. These PNGs are loaded at runtime through the mod-aware asset resolver; they are not a writable global asset collection. A mod can replace a base image by providing the same relative path under its own `assets/` directory. The enabled mod with the highest priority wins, with stable mod ID as the deterministic tie-break. The wood floor is a first-pass treatment for the Old Shrine prototype, not a claim that every location should use wood.

The interface uses named PNG icons from the bundled `icons/ui/` collection for actions, menu controls, conditions, records, and combat choices. The first NPC/corpse world markers reuse the existing character/remains icons as tinted sprites; dedicated actor art and richer NPC silhouettes remain future asset work. The world clock uses a separate large celestial-art atlas at `world/celestial/sun-moon.png`; the left panel contains the sun and the right panel contains the moon. The gameplay renderer crops these regions into large sky sprites, keeping them out of the 32×32 icon collection. Both icon and world-art paths use the mod-aware asset resolver, so a mod can replace either at the same relative path. Text separators and ordinary labels use simple ASCII punctuation instead of decorative glyphs that may be missing from the default font. Mod-owned assets live under the installed mod's `assets/` directory and can be referenced explicitly with `mod:<manifest-id>/<relative-path>.png`.

## Spatial HUD

The local exploration screen keeps a compact character-status panel in the header. It reads the active character's name, level, current/maximum health, and current conditions directly from authoritative game state. The shared health-gauge primitive supplies both the blood-fill indicator and numeric values. The conditions line names active conditions and explicitly says when none are present. The location title and contextual feedback remain separate from this panel, and the spatial menu control and Android-only virtual joystick retain their existing roles. The former Leave control and Gameplay dashboard transition have been retired; the menu control opens the pause/navigation overlay on tap and the developer console on a 650 ms touch hold without dragging.

This is the first HUD layer, not the complete reference layout. Combat telegraphs, enemy vitality, ability controls, a minimap/compass, and local ally orders are not implemented by this change.

## Player animation

The spatial renderer uses a renderer-independent animation player driven by the mod-aware `spatial/player_oathbound_animated.json` manifest. The sheet is a row-major 6-column × 8-row atlas of 32×48 frames (192×384 total). Direction rows are paired in north, east, south, and west order: each direction has one four-frame looping idle row followed by one six-frame looping walk row. The idle rows use columns 0–3; columns 4–5 are transparent and unused. The walk rows use all six columns. The manifest defines frame dimensions, atlas dimensions, clip rows, starting columns, frame counts, frame durations, and looping behavior, so the renderer does not duplicate animation layout or timing assumptions.

Idle playback advances while the player is stationary at 0.25 seconds per frame. Walking starts at 0.12 seconds per frame at full movement input; lower movement input slows playback without changing authoritative movement speed or collision rules. Playback accumulates elapsed time rather than update count. The four directions are the screen-facing poses for the three-quarter view. Blocked movement can update facing without starting a walk cycle, releasing movement returns to the matching directional idle cycle, and repeated stop/pause calls do not restart an already-playing idle cycle. Player feet remain anchored to the ground position; animation dimensions affect only sprite presentation, never the collision footprint or interaction rules.

The manifest and PNG use the same mod-aware asset resolver. The manifest is validated before use; a missing or invalid manifest falls back to the built-in 32×48 / 6×8 defaults. The PNG must match the dimensions declared by the manifest (192×384 for the base-game contract). Until `spatial/player_oathbound_animated.png` contains valid artwork, the renderer keeps using the existing 16×24 `spatial/player_oathbound.png` static fallback. Missing or invalid art does not prevent spatial exploration.

## Camera behavior

The camera uses a viewport-scaled dead zone: ordinary small player movements do not move the camera, while crossing the dead-zone edge shifts the target by only the excess distance. Exponential smoothing keeps the follow response consistent across frame rates. The resulting camera position is clamped after smoothing as well as at the target, so a stale/out-of-bounds camera position cannot temporarily expose space beyond the projected map after a map or viewport change. Maps smaller than the viewport are centered.

When the player's current grid cell belongs to an authored room, the camera target is additionally constrained by that room's projected bounds. The bounds include the floor footprint and visible wall-top height. A room smaller than the viewport is centered rather than forcing the camera to chase the player around an area the viewport cannot frame independently. Cells not assigned to a room retain the map-wide camera behavior, which preserves the existing treatment of open corridors and older saves with no room topology.

Crossing an open interior door changes the active room framing region. The camera continues to use the same frame-rate-independent exponential follow, and only the target changes; the interpolated camera is not clamped to the destination room on each frame, avoiding an instantaneous snap at a doorway. Closed and locked doors remain governed by the authoritative movement/pathfinding rules, so the player cannot trigger a room transition by crossing a blocked edge.

Room framing currently uses the axis-aligned projected bounds of a room's cell set. This is a soft framing constraint, not visibility culling; irregular room footprints can still contain empty space inside their bounding rectangle. The map-wide bounds remain the final safety clamp.

## Validation status and next gates

Automated unit tests cover deterministic map/prop/actor placement, reachable actor positions, authored chamber geometry and doorway access, room/exit reachability, map serialization and current-schema validation, reachable physical exits with prop-free corridors, projection offsets, inverse input mapping, continuous movement and low-frame-rate movement recovery, terrain/prop-footprint collision, interaction range and prop-blocked line of sight, contextual door open/close actions, room topology validation, closed-door pathfinding, contextual target priority, spatial-overlay state separation, pause-menu action routing, dead-zone behavior, frame-rate-independent camera following and post-smoothing map bounds, viewport-aware radial wheel sizing and entry placement, eased wheel selection feedback, wall-top/prop depth ordering, selected-folder PNG dimensions, and UI icon fallbacks.

These tests are source-level coverage. They do not establish successful end-to-end play on desktop or Android; interactive testing and on-device verification are intentionally left to the repository owner.

The first representative authored room now exists as the Old Shrine chamber template. Manual visual and interaction playtesting was tracked under [#446](https://github.com/Golden-Bastion/the-ever-unfolding-saga/issues/446), which is now closed. Its issue history does not contain recorded test results, so closure alone should not be interpreted as documented verification of every visual acceptance criterion. The original validation scope covered directional idle/walk playback and alignment, movement around both sides of the Old Shrine pillar and table, doorway traversal, wall-top/face ordering, camera framing, and desktop and Android landscape controls.

Integrated validation of the spatial-first menu, World Map, dedicated-screen returns, and migrated gameplay functions was tracked under [#421](https://github.com/Golden-Bastion/the-ever-unfolding-saga/issues/421), which is also closed. Automated tests establish model reachability and depth calculations but do not replace visual playtesting. Room-aware camera transitions, broader authored layouts, and richer room/door editing workflows remain future work and are tracked centrally in the [roadmap](../../ROADMAP.md).


## Room and interior-door topology

Rooms are renderer-neutral sets of walkable grid cells with stable IDs. A cell belongs to at most one room; unassigned cells remain valid corridors or outdoor space. Interior doors occupy a canonical edge between two cardinally adjacent cells and name the two rooms they connect. This keeps a doorway from consuming either floor tile and supports irregular room footprints.

An open door permits movement and pathfinding across its edge. Closed and locked doors block both. Closing or opening changes the door record; a locked door must first be explicitly unlocked, which returns it to the closed state. Key ownership and lock-picking rules are intentionally outside this topology layer. Door artwork is presentation-specific and is not part of this foundation; the renderer can map the authoritative state to open/closed visuals when door assets are available. Map-edge `MapExit` passages remain separate and continue to represent travel between world locations.
